Menlo Park
The epicenter of venture capital and tech money on the Peninsula
$2.5M – $10M+ Established, leafy, prestigious VC partners, tech execs, old money
Key neighborhoods
Allied Arts
$3M – $7M
Quiet, tree-lined, classic Menlo Park. Large lots, craftsman and ranch homes. Highly walkable to downtown. This is the neighborhood people picture when they say "Menlo Park."
Sand Hill Road, the address of more VC firms than anywhere on earth, is a 5-minute drive. That proximity matters to a certain buyer.
West Menlo Park
$3.5M – $8M+
The premium pocket west of El Camino. Borders Atherton and Las Lomitas school district. Bigger lots, more privacy, more old-money feel than central Menlo.
West Menlo is often described as "Atherton adjacent." Buyers who can't afford Atherton but want that feel end up here. Las Lomitas school district is a massive price driver.
University Heights
$1.2M – $5M+
A pocket within West Menlo, straddling Alameda de las Pulgas. Unincorporated San Mateo County, so it has a distinct country feel: no sidewalks, smaller lots, older ranches giving way to new two-story builds. Same top schools as West Menlo.
Because it's unincorporated county, not the City of Menlo Park, building and remodeling rules are generally less restrictive: a real draw for buyers who want to build. Las Lomitas / La Entrada schools drive the premium.
Sharon Heights
$2.5M – $5M
More suburban and spread out. Home to the Sharon Heights Golf Club. A quieter, more residential feel: less walkable but spacious. Popular with buyers who want room and quiet over urban proximity.
Sharon Heights Golf Club is a private club, membership by invitation. Mentioning it signals neighborhood familiarity to buyers in that world.
The Willows
$2.5M – $4.5M
South of downtown, denser, more modest lot sizes. More accessible entry point into Menlo Park. Popular with younger buyers and families. Feels more relaxed than West Menlo.
The Willows is in the Menlo Park City School District (MPCSD), not Las Lomitas. That distinction matters and will come up from informed buyers.
Vintage Oaks
$2.5M – $4.5M
A small, established neighborhood dating to the 1940s on the east side, near downtown and the Willows. Smaller, walkable, more modest homes than West Menlo. A more accessible way into Menlo Park with a tight community feel.
Vintage Oaks is in MPCSD (Laurel/Encinal Elementary, Hillview Middle), not Las Lomitas: same east-of-El-Camino pattern as the Willows. Residents are known for a beloved neighborhood dog park.
Stanford Weekend Acres
$2M – $5M+
A tiny, semi-rural pocket of just seven looping streets tucked between Alpine Road, the Stanford Golf Course, and San Francisquito Creek, right against the Stanford Dish trails. Easy to miss. A mix of older humble homes and newer expanded builds.
On the MLS it usually shows under the alias "Alpine Road," not Stanford Weekend Acres, so listings here are easy to overlook: knowing that alias is a real local edge. Unincorporated county (looser building rules), feeding top Las Lomitas / La Entrada schools.
One thing to always know
Menlo Park has two distinct school districts: Las Lomitas ESD (covers West Menlo, University Heights, Stanford Weekend Acres, Allied Arts, and areas west of El Camino) and MPCSD (covers central and eastern Menlo Park including the Willows and Vintage Oaks). Buyers will ask. You must know which side of El Camino a listing falls on: it can mean a $500K+ price difference for the same house.
Palo Alto
Stanford's backyard and the highest price-per-sqft on the Peninsula
$2.8M – $12M+ Academic, walkable, intense Professors, founders, engineers, Stanford affiliates
Key neighborhoods
Old Palo Alto
$5M – $12M+
The most prestigious address in the city. Older, estate-scale homes on large lots. Quiet, private, and walkable to University Ave. The benchmark for Palo Alto pricing.
When someone says "I want Palo Alto" and has $8M+ to spend, this is almost always what they mean. University Ave restaurants are walkable: that matters to this buyer.
Midtown
$3M – $5.5M
Central, family-friendly, more approachable than Old PA. Good schools, decent lot sizes, strong resale. The "reliable" neighborhood: not flashy but consistently in demand.
Midtown is popular with Stanford faculty and long-tenured tech employees who want a stable, family-friendly base over status. Strong repeat-buyer profile.
Professorville
$4M – $9M
Historic district near downtown. Victorian and craftsman architecture. Named for Stanford professors who built here in the early 1900s. Walkable, charming, architecturally distinct.
Professorville is a historic district, so renovations require design review. Some buyers love the character; others hate the restrictions. Always mention this upfront.
South Palo Alto / Barron Park
$2.8M – $4.5M
More affordable entry into PAUSD. Quieter, more suburban feel. Barron Park is known for a relaxed, community-oriented vibe. Good for buyers priced out of Old PA or Midtown.
Barron Park has a herd of donkeys that live at a local stable. Locals love them and they're a real community touchstone. Knowing that detail signals you know the area.
One thing to always know
PAUSD (Palo Alto Unified) is the main school district and commands a massive premium. But watch for the edges: some South Palo Alto streets feed into Los Altos School District instead, which buyers in the know will test you on. Verify district boundaries before you open your mouth about schools.
Atherton
The wealthiest zip code in America: no downtown, no commercial, just estates
$5M – $30M+ Ultra-private, estate, old money Billionaires, tech founders, hedge fund, inherited wealth
Key areas (Atherton has no official neighborhoods: it functions as one unified enclave)
West Atherton
$8M – $30M+
The most private and prestigious section. Very large lots, gated driveways, extreme seclusion. Trophy properties and generational estates. The highest price tier in the city.
Properties here rarely come to market publicly. Many trade off-market. If you're showing a buyer something in West Atherton, it's a serious transaction.
Central Atherton
$5M – $12M
Still exceptional but slightly more accessible than the west side. Mix of original estates and newer construction. Atherton's "entry point," which means it starts at $5M.
Buyers who say "I want Atherton" but have $5–6M are looking here. A good comp here can still clear $1,500/sqft. Knowing that number cold impresses clients.
Atherton / Menlo border
$4M – $8M
The eastern edge blending into Menlo Park. Same school district access (Las Lomitas), slightly lower price than deep Atherton. Popular with buyers who want the feel without the top-tier price.
Listings here sometimes market themselves as "Atherton-adjacent" or even just "Atherton" informally. Always verify the actual city boundary before presenting to a client.
Lindenwood
$5M – $15M
A gated community within Atherton. HOA governs the road maintenance. Exceptionally private. One of the most recognized addresses even within Atherton.
Lindenwood has private roads maintained by residents. Some buyers love the extra layer of privacy; others see HOA involvement as a negative. Know before you show.
One thing to always know
Atherton has no commercial zone: no cafes, no restaurants, no shops. It's entirely residential by design. The city's identity is built around privacy and exclusion. Buyers coming from urban environments sometimes don't realize this upfront. Mention it early so there are no surprises.
Redwood City
The most accessible and diverse entry point into Peninsula real estate
$1.4M – $4M+ Energetic, diverse, up-and-coming First-time Peninsula buyers, young families, investors
Key neighborhoods
Farm Hill / Emerald Hills
$2M – $4M
Elevated hillside neighborhoods with views, larger lots, and a quieter suburban feel. More space for the money than flat Redwood City. Popular with buyers wanting privacy without Atherton prices.
Emerald Hills has winding roads and a rural feel despite being minutes from 280. Buyers who discover it often say it doesn't feel like what they expected from "Redwood City."
Mount Carmel
$1.6M – $2.8M
Central, walkable, close to downtown and Caltrain. A mix of older craftsman and post-war homes. Strong rental demand. Good entry-level for Peninsula buyers.
Downtown Redwood City has transformed significantly in the past decade: more restaurants, nightlife, and office workers. Mount Carmel buyers benefit from that without paying downtown premiums.
Redwood Shores
$1.4M – $2.5M
A planned waterfront community on the bay. Condos, townhomes, and single-family homes. More affordable, very popular with commuters to Oracle HQ (located there) and other bay-side tech campuses.
Redwood Shores has a lagoon system: some homes are waterfront and command a premium. Those listings sell fast. It's technically in Redwood City but feels like its own world.
Kentfield Commons / Roy Cloud area
$2.2M – $3.5M
Gated community feel, very family-oriented, near the Atherton border. Roy Cloud elementary is a draw. Quieter and more suburban than central Redwood City.
The Atherton adjacency here, similar to West Menlo, adds a psychological premium that shows up in prices. Buyers feel like they're in that world without paying Atherton prices.
One thing to always know
Redwood City feeds into Sequoia Union High School District for high school (Woodside High and Sequoia High), but elementary varies by neighborhood. Roy Cloud, Hoover, and Kennedy are the names that come up. Woodside High has a strong reputation: knowing that helps you redirect school conversations away from weaker elementary ratings.
San Carlos
"The City of Good Living": the Peninsula's most beloved family town
$1.5M – $3.5M Charming, walkable, tight-knit Young families, move-up buyers, community-oriented
Key neighborhoods
White Oaks
$2.2M – $3.5M
The most desirable neighborhood in San Carlos. Tree-lined streets, larger lots, pride-of-ownership homes. Short walk to Laurel Street. The address families work toward in San Carlos.
Homes in White Oaks often sell with multiple offers regardless of market conditions. If your buyer wants San Carlos and has the budget, start here: it holds value exceptionally well.
Brittan Acres
$1.8M – $2.8M
Flat, grid-pattern streets west of El Camino. Very family-friendly, close to Burton Park. One of the most kid-friendly pockets in the city, with a high density of young families.
Burton Park is the center of community life here: Saturday little league, picnics, community events. Mentioning it tells buyers you know what daily life actually looks like in this neighborhood.
Howard Park
$1.6M – $2.5M
East of El Camino, closer to the bay. More modest and affordable entry into San Carlos. Mix of older ranch homes and some newer builds. Good for buyers stretching into the market.
East of El Camino generally means lower prices anywhere on the Peninsula. That's a rule of thumb worth internalizing: it applies to San Carlos, Menlo Park, and Redwood City alike.
Central San Carlos / Laurel St area
$1.7M – $3M
Walking distance to Laurel Street's restaurants, shops, and coffee. The "downtown adjacent" pocket. Buyers who want urban walkability without going to Redwood City choose this.
Laurel Street is a legitimate dining destination, not just a strip mall. San Carlos residents take pride in it. Knowing a few restaurant names (like The Refuge for beer and sandwiches) signals real local knowledge.
One thing to always know
San Carlos Elementary SD covers K–8. High school goes to Carlmont High School via Sequoia Union HSD. Carlmont is physically located in Belmont (not San Carlos): knowing that geography prevents confusion when buyers look it up on a map and wonder why the high school address doesn't match the city.
Belmont
Underrated hillside city: Carlmont High's actual home and a quiet value play
$1.3M – $2.8M Quiet, hillside, residential Practical buyers, value seekers, families wanting space
Key neighborhoods
Belmont Hills
$1.8M – $2.8M+
The premium pocket. Elevated, with bay views and canyon views. Larger lots. More dramatic landscape than flat Peninsula neighborhoods. The place buyers end up when they discover Belmont.
Views from Belmont Hills on a clear day include the Bay, the Dumbarton Bridge, and on exceptional days the East Bay hills. Always check the weather before showing hillside homes here.
Old Belmont / Central
$1.3M – $2.2M
Flat to gently rolling. Older neighborhood stock, quiet streets. The most affordable and accessible part of Belmont. Good for buyers who want a single-family home without a steep driveway.
Many buyers discover Belmont after being outbid in San Carlos repeatedly. The pitch: same Carlmont High, same Sequoia Union HSD, $200–300K less. That's a compelling value case to make.
Belmont Village
$1.4M – $2M
Near Belmont's small downtown strip. Walkable to the Caltrain station, which is a draw for SF commuters. More urban density, smaller lots, but convenient location.
Caltrain access here is underrated for SF commuters who can't afford to live in the city. The commute is clean: 35–40 min to SF by express. Lean into this with that buyer profile.
Rancho Cielo / Carlmont area
$1.5M – $2.5M
Near Carlmont High School itself. Family-oriented, convenient to Caltrain and 101. Comfortable suburban pocket that benefits from the high school's proximity and reputation.
Buyers with high schoolers specifically seek walkability to Carlmont. Mentioning which streets are within walking distance is a detail that earns trust with that buyer type.
One thing to always know
Belmont's elementary and middle school falls under Belmont-Redwood Shores SD (K–8). This is the same district that covers Redwood Shores: buyers sometimes get confused when they see the district name and think Redwood Shores, not Belmont. High school is Carlmont via Sequoia Union HSD.
